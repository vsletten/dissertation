Abstract

A 1999 dissertation proposed that the macroscopic kinetics of a dissolving mineral could be computed rather than fitted: price every elementary surface reaction with quantum mechanics, hand the prices to a kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC) simulation of the crystal lattice, and let dissolution rates, etch-pit textures and apparent activation energies emerge from the statistics of countable events. The quantum half was unaffordable at the time. This report documents the system that now exists in its place, built between 2026-08-12 and 2026-08-22 on one workstation: petra, a chemistry-free lattice simulation engine in which the mineral, its state vocabulary, its reactions and its update rule are all declared in a single TOML document, and quarry, a GPU-accelerated transition-state-theory pipeline that turns a crystal structure and a reaction taxonomy into site-resolved barriers with per-number provenance and emits them in petra's own format.

We describe the theory both tools rest on (transition-state theory, the master equation and its exact sampling, linear-elastic defect energetics, quasi-rigid-rotor thermochemistry with tunnelling corrections), the algorithms and data structures that make them fast and reproducible (two-level Fenwick selection, influence-radius incremental updates, salted RNG streams under a bitwise determinism law, partial-Hessian vibrational analysis on mechanically embedded clusters), and the verification ladders that make their answers trustworthy (analytic gates, site-by-site golden gates against the 1999 model, three conformance decks with closed-form answers, saddle-point gates that reject plausible wrong answers). The final part records, separately, the four research programs currently running on the platform: silicate dissolution and the laboratory–field rate discrepancy, pitting corrosion, grain-surface astrochemistry, and argon release from muscovite.

Background

The idea, its lineage, the problem it was built to attack, and the piece of hardware that made it affordable. Everything in this part is shared by all four research tracks; from Part II onward the tools are described as tools.

Section 1The architecture in one figure

Every chemical change at a surface is an event: a bond breaks, a proton hops, an ion detaches. Transition-state theory prices each event with a rate constant; the master equation says how a population of sites evolves under those prices; kinetic Monte Carlo samples that equation exactly. The platform is the pipe that connects the pricing to the sampling.

Figure 1 is the whole system. On the left, quarry takes a fragment of the crystal, locates the saddle point of a reaction on it, verifies that the saddle is the right one, and reduces the result to three numbers: an activation enthalpy, an activation entropy, and the imaginary frequency of the reaction mode.The three numbers, not a rate constant. Quarry uses CODATA 2018 constants; petra inherits the dissertation's truncated gas constant, 1.987×10⁻³ kcal mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, and the two differ by 0.17 %. A unit test in quarry asserts that a rate computed both ways agrees to 3×10⁻³ and disagrees at 10⁻⁴, which is why barriers, never pre-exponentiated rates, cross the bridge. Those numbers are written into a TOML deck with a provenance comment. On the right, petra compiles the deck into dense tables and runs an exact continuous-time Markov chain over a lattice of sites: draw a site in proportion to its total propensity, fire one of its events, advance the clock by an exponentially distributed waiting time, repair only the propensities the event could have changed, repeat. Observables (populations, rate spectra, surface areas, exposure ages) are sampled from the trajectory and aggregated across seeded replicas.

Two properties of the design carry most of its weight. First, the deck is the model: petra's engine contains no chemistry names, so a kaolinite deck, a stainless-steel passive film, an interstellar ice mantle and a muscovite sheet are the same kind of object to it. Second, everything downstream of the deck is reproducible bit for bit. Given the same deck, seed and update strategy, petra produces the same trajectory on any machine, forever; that contract is a test, not a goal. Quarry's numbers are not deterministic in that sense (an SCF converges where it converges), but every number it emits is tied to a content hash of the geometry it came from, and every campaign writes its failures down with the same care as its successes.

Section 3The problem: the field is a thousand times slower than the lab

Silicate minerals dissolve two to five orders of magnitude more slowly in nature than in the laboratory when the rates are normalised the same way. This is one of the oldest unresolved quantitative problems in geochemistry, and the platform's flagship scientific target. It is described here because it motivates several engine features that would otherwise look arbitrary (rate-spectrum and exposure-age observables, two separate surface-area measures, finite defect inventories), not because the tracks share it as a result.

The canonical framing is White and Brantley's 2003 synthesis: the gap is not a constant offset but a decline of rate with the age of the reacting surface. Fresh Panola granite in laboratory columns declined parabolically over six years to 7.0×10⁻¹⁴ mol m⁻² s⁻¹; naturally pre-weathered granite from the same outcrop ran at 2.1×10⁻¹⁵; extrapolating the fresh-material decline implied thousands of years to reach the field rate. A follow-on experiment kept four granitoids dissolving in flow-through columns for 13.8 years, the longest weathering experiment ever run, and the rates were still falling at the end. Meanwhile BET surface area rises as reactivity falls, an internal contradiction for any model in which rate is proportional to area.White & Brantley call this the time-dependent roughness factor. Petra's surface_area observable therefore reports two numbers, a projected geometric area and a BET-like exposed-site count, and never divides one by the other on the user's behalf.

Two families of explanation compete. The extrinsic family (Maher; Maher and Chamberlain) says the field is slow because pore fluids sit near equilibrium; the mineral surface is largely irrelevant. The intrinsic family says fresh, ground, defect-rich laboratory surfaces are simply more reactive than ancient ones whose reactive sites were spent long ago: a hypothesis about the statistical state of the lattice, which a continuum rate law with one k and one A structurally cannot represent and a defect-resolved KMC tracks natively. Fischer, Arvidson and Lüttge's rate-spectrum work made the intrinsic view measurable: the rate of a surface is a distribution over sites, and normalising by area throws away the variance that carries the mechanism. Brantley's 2025 review lists five unresolved categories, of which the first is "changing mineral surface attributes difficult to model", and no quantitative attribution exists: nobody can say how many of the missing log units are surface aging.

The stakes stopped being academic when enhanced rock weathering became an industry. Spreading crushed silicate on cropland to draw down CO₂ puts freshly ground feedstock, the maximally lab-like surface, into the field and waits for it to age; published removal estimates from field trials span four orders of magnitude, which is the discrepancy replayed with carbon credits attached.

One published result constrains any defect-based story and is treated in this program as a validation gate rather than an objection. Blum, Yund and Lasaga (1990) raised quartz dislocation density from below 10⁵ to 5×10¹⁰ cm⁻² and found far-from-equilibrium dissolution rates indistinguishable despite dramatic etch pitting. A correct model must therefore show defects controlling rates near equilibrium and in the fast tail of the rate spectrum, while leaving the far-from-equilibrium mean alone. Track A's plan is built around reproducing that two-regime behaviour before claiming anything about the field.

Section 4The hardware inversion

In the 1990s a single barrier calculation on a realistic aluminosilicate fragment cost weeks of a DEC Alpha; Xiao and Lasaga's founding disilicate and Si–O–Al dimer studies (15–25 atoms, Hartree–Fock and MP2 in small bases) monopolised one of the group's two machines for about a year. The workstation this program runs on is a 16-core Ryzen 9 7950X with 64 GB of memory and an RTX 4090. The interesting fact is not that it is roughly a thousand times faster in raw CPU FLOPS; it is which operation the GPU accelerates.

II

Petra, the engine

A chemistry-free lattice simulation engine. The crystal, its state vocabulary, its reactions and the rule for advancing time are all data. This part describes the deck format, the compiled runtime, the exact-sampling algorithm and its data structures, the defect and schedule machinery, and the verification ladder. Six Rust crates: petra-core (runtime), petra-deck (schema and compiler), petra-observables, petra-io, petra-cli, petra-wasm.

Section 5The deck is a document

A petra deck is a declarative spatial stochastic state machine: a set of sites with fixed adjacency, a finite vocabulary of states per site, guarded local rewrite rules with numerical weights, and an update strategy that says how a rule is selected and how time advances. Continuous-time kinetic Monte Carlo is one strategy among four.

The schema (version 2, RFC-001) splits the document into four orthogonal sections. [structure] declares the site graph: either an explicit crystallographic unit cell with fractional coordinates, bonds and periodic image offsets, tiled to a supercell, or a grid shorthand (square, hexagonal or cubic, von Neumann or Moore neighbourhoods) that compiles to the same adjacency representation. [dynamics] declares the rules and the thermodynamic environment. [execution] selects the strategy, stopping conditions, an optional temperature schedule and the ensemble policy. [observables] declares what is sampled. The principle is that a strategy change must not touch structure or dynamics, and an observable change must not touch execution. Version-1 decks (the flat layout the kaolinite and Kossel examples were written in) load unchanged through a pure load-time transform that implies strategy = "ctmc"; a test compiles every shipped v1 deck through the shim and through an explicit v2 rewrite and asserts the two compiled tables are identical.Every struct in the schema is deny_unknown_fields, so a deck that declares schema = 2 and then uses flat v1 keys fails with the unknown field named. A second gate demands eight bit-identical steps between a v1 engine and its v2 rewrite.

A representative fragment, from the Track E muscovite deck, shows most of the vocabulary in a dozen lines: a labelled bond, a reaction whose guard reads through that label, an Eyring rate, a tabulated environment modifier and two effect targets.

Section 6Structure: from unit cell to site graph

A unit cell is a fractional-to-Cartesian matrix plus a list of template sites (kind, fractional or Cartesian position) and bonds. A bond is a triple (i, j, Δcell): template site i is bonded to the image of template site j displaced by Δcell = (da, db, dc) cells. Bonds may carry a label, which generalises the legacy model's special-cased "pair" oxygen into ordinary addressable topology. The compiler expands every bond onto both endpoints with the mirrored offset, and a reciprocity check verifies that every i→(j, Δ) has its j→(i, −Δ) twin with the same label; a failure there is classified as a compiler bug, not a user error.The matrix form exists because the legacy kaolinite cell is a sheared cell that conventional (a b c α β γ) parameters cannot express verbatim. Columns are lattice vectors; quarry reads the same matrix with the same convention when it cuts clusters (Section 18).

The lattice tiles the cell na × nb × nc times. Site indices are flat, template index varying fastest: i = ((a·nb + b)·nc + c)·ntmpl + t, with an exact inverse. Each axis carries its own boundary condition. Periodic wraps by Euclidean remainder. Open drops every bond that crosses the face: a free surface. Fixed also drops the bond, and additionally marks any site in the terminal layer that lost a bond at that fixed cut as frozen: it participates in its neighbours' environments but never hosts an event, and an effect that tries to write it fails closed. The distinction is subtle enough to have its own test: a site that lost a bond to an open face on a different axis is a surface site, not an anchor. This reproduces the 1999 model's EDGE termination, a ragged frozen wall standing in for bulk crystal beyond the box.

Adjacency is stored compressed-sparse-row: an offset table and a flat neighbour array, with a parallel array of bond labels. A site has however many neighbours its chemistry gives it (2, 4, 6 or 26), there are no sentinel slots, and the out-of-bounds phantom that shaped the legacy model's dynamics is unrepresentable. Grid decks compile to exactly the same structure through a one-site unit cell with the family's offset vectors; a post-tiling simplifier removes self-loops and duplicate edges so that a 2×2 periodic Moore grid gets degree 3 rather than double-counting its own images.

Initial conditions are build passes. Each [[init]] entry sweeps sites in index order (or an explicit sorted site list), filters by region slab, kind, state set and guards, optionally draws a Bernoulli trial against a probability from a salted initialisation RNG stream, and applies a state operation, possibly repeated once per matching neighbour and possibly changing the site's kind. Writes are visible to later passes and later sites within a pass, which is the legacy surface-termination convention. The kaolinite deck uses eighteen passes to carve the slab, terminate the surface and count hydroxyls; a substitution deck uses one pass with probability = 0.02 to scatter iron over aluminium sites; the ice-mantle deck uses one pass to freeze 20 % of sites into a deeper binding class. The substitution gate checks the binomial count on a 40×40 sheet (mean 400, accepted 313–487, ±5σ) and that the same seed reproduces the same lattice.

Section 7State: kind, occupant, configuration

The dynamic state of a site is a pair (occupant, configuration): the species sitting there (or vacant) and a deck-named local arrangement such as "2OH", "hydrolyzed" or "pr1". Each site kind declares its allowed states. At compile time every (kind, occupant, configuration) triple is interned in declaration order to a dense StateId(u16), every state set mentioned by a rule becomes a bitset (a Vec<u64>; membership is one word load and a mask), and the runtime never distinguishes occupant from configuration again. The legacy three-digit codes map directly: 103 is Al_oct with occupant Al and configuration "2OH"; 301 and 302 are the intact and hydrolysed states of the Si–O–Si bridging oxygen; 100 is a vacant aluminium site.

Keeping occupant separate from configuration is the design-ahead for defects. A rule may match "any trivalent occupant of an octahedral site", so an iron-substituted deck reuses every rule that does not care about the occupant and adds narrower rules for the ones that do. Two consequences of contiguous per-kind ids are used directly. A kind's states form a ladder, so the legacy protonation counter (state++) is a bounds-checked integer add, shift = ±n, and falling off the ladder is a runtime stop rather than silent corruption; declaration order is therefore semantically meaningful, and the deck-authoring notes say so. And the legacy model's hidden per-site memory of which aluminium holds a proton becomes two ordinary states, Osa.pr1 and Osa.pr2, addressed through two bond labels: hidden mutable state becomes visible, exportable topology.

Names resolve three ways: a plain or kind-qualified state name, an @alias that expands recursively (depth capped at four), or "*", every declared state, which under a kind restriction turns a guard into a pure coordination counter. An ambiguous bare name inside a restricted scope is a compile error that names the scope.

Section 8Rules: guarded local rewrites

An elementary reaction is a center selector, a conjunction of guards over the neighbourhood, a rate, an ordered list of modifiers, and effects. The center selector is a kind plus a state set. A guard is a neighbour selector with a count window: at least min and at most max neighbours matching the selector. The selector has six orthogonal filters: graph distance (exactly 1 or exactly 2), kind, bond label (distance 1 only), an excluded bond label, frozen-or-live, and a state set. Distance 2 means exactly two hops, excluding the center and every distance-1 site; the surface-reachability rule of the kaolinite model ("at least one second neighbour is hydrolysed") is one guard.

Three selector features carry unusual weight. Labels let a rule address a specific bond partner ("the oxygen on the pair bond") rather than a class of neighbours. Excluded labels make bonds invisible to a selector on both hops of a distance-2 walk, so distances are measured on the filtered graph. This is how the multilayer kaolinite deck keeps surface reachability from propagating across an anhydrous interlayer contact: hydrogen bonds are real bonds for energetics but carry no reactive medium, so the reachability guard excludes their label.The test for this is unusually careful. Stripping the exclusion from a distance-2 guard is not a valid control, because exact-distance-2 already excludes the distance-1 acceptor. The honest positive control probes at distance 1, where the hydrogen-bond edge is real and its acceptor is hydrolysed (count 1), then re-applies the exclusion (count 0). Frozen lets a guard see or ignore the fixed boundary wall.

Effects name a target and an operation. Targets are the center, a source (every vacant site of a kind, for flux-driven deposition), the first matching neighbour in site-id order (zero matches is a hard error), one uniformly random matching neighbour (matches are sorted by site id before the draw, so the choice cannot depend on adjacency traversal order), or all matching neighbours. Operations are set, shift along the state ladder, and map from old state to new with a deck flag deciding whether an unmapped state is an error or a skip, which generalises the legacy adsorption-fatal, desorption-silent asymmetry. A rule may declare weighted branches instead of a single effect list, generalising the legacy "which side keeps the proton" coin flip; a rule with one branch consumes no random draw, which matters for bitwise parity.

Source rules have no center. The compiler infers one from the effects' target states: they must all name one kind, and the synthesised center set is every vacant state of that kind. A bath event thereby becomes one independent per-vacant-site event with no special engine path, and the total deposition rate falls with coverage for free.

Section 9Rate physics

All energies are converted to kcal/mol at compile time and all rates are evaluated in f64. The runtime gas constant is the dissertation's R = 1.987×10⁻³ kcal mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and kB/h = 2.0836612×10¹⁰ s⁻¹ K⁻¹. A rule's base rate is one of three expressions:

k0 = k  (constant)k0 = A e−Ea/RT  (Arrhenius)k0 = kBTh eΔS‡/R e−ΔH‡/RT  (Eyring)

The complete resolved rate, as implemented in one line of the reaction module, multiplies the base rate by a solution-coupling factor, an environment factor from the modifiers, and an optional strain factor:

k = k0(T) · ∏X aX eΔμX/RT · exp[−(s·ui + Σm ΔEa,m(nm)) / RT] · ∏m fm

The product over X runs over species the rule consumes from solution, with activity aX and chemical potential ΔμX from the thermo section: the standard supersaturation factor for attachment, and the generalisation of the legacy adsorption law. It is folded at compile time into a single log-space term, ln a + Δμ/RT, and materialised rather than recomputed so that the isothermal hot path keeps its historical floating-point order when temperature schedules landed. The solution is an infinite reservoir in v1; a depleting reservoir is roadmap.

Modifiers adjust the barrier per local environment, each keyed on a neighbour count n from a selector. by_count is a tabulated ΔEa indexed by n, the last entry extending to all higher counts; it is the general primitive because barriers are rarely linear in coordination. per_match is the linear convenience, bond-counting in the Brønsted–Evans–Polanyi sense. when { min, max } is a discrete override that adds a ΔEa or multiplies by a factor when the count falls in a window. The eV-unit test pins the point: on an open chain with dea = [0.0, 0.1, 0.5], end sites with one neighbour get 0.1 eV and interior sites with two get 0.5, not 0.2.

Reversibility and detailed balance are designed rather than built: a rule may declare a reverse with reaction energy ΔE, from which the compiler would generate the inverse rule with k⁻ = k⁺·exp(ΔE/RT) and lint every closed thermodynamic cycle in each kind's state graph for ΣΔE ≈ 0. The strain coupling already follows the convention the linter would check (Section 12). The Metropolis strategy reuses exactly the same count machinery but sums modifier contributions into an energy difference rather than an exponent.

Section 10Update strategies

The engine core owns the lattice, neighbourhood queries, rule matching, the RNG streams and trajectory recording. A strategy owns selection and time: given read access to the current state and a handle for applying transitions, it decides what fires and how the clock advances. Four strategies implement the trait, selected by one deck field; the same deck can run under a different one by changing that field, which is how the conformance decks of Section 14 test generality.

Two implementation facts are worth knowing. The synchronous automaton gets its double-buffer semantics without a second buffer: the handle a strategy receives holds an immutable reference to the lattice and only queues prepared transitions, so every rule match reads pre-step state by construction and the core commits the batch afterwards; the type system enforces the cellular-automaton contract. And batch commits are fail-closed, not last-writer-wins: two transitions writing the same site to different states abort the step before anything is mutated, and the gate asserts the lattice is byte-identical afterwards. A test builds a working exact-CTMC strategy from nothing but the public trait surface and shows it matches the built-in bit for bit, which is the proof that the seam is complete; another test uses a one-off "fire this named rule at the first eligible site" strategy to prove every diffusion and recombination rule in the ice-mantle deck is reachable.

Tau-leaping is named and reserved but not built. It is an acceleration of the CTMC (a leap condition on propensity change, Poisson counts per rule over a leap τ, a rejection pass for negative populations) rather than a new strategy, and the batch-capable apply handle is the hook it will need.

Section 11Inside a CTMC step

Rejection-free kinetic Monte Carlo samples the master equation exactly: the next event is drawn with probability proportional to its propensity, and the waiting time is exponential with rate equal to the total propensity. Petra's implementation is the n-fold way with sites as the groups and a Fenwick tree replacing the linear class scan.

The loop is short enough to write out. Let R = Σi Σr ki,r be the total propensity. Draw u, descend the Fenwick tree with target u·R to a site in O(log N), then scan that site's short cached event list with the descent's residual to pick the reaction. Draw u′ and advance time by

Δt = −ln(1 − u′) / R

(the 1 − u′ maps [0, 1) onto (0, 1] so the logarithm never sees zero). Apply the transition, drawing for branches and one-of-many effects in effect order. Then repair: collect the sites whose state actually changed (plus the center if nothing changed, so a no-op event still refreshes itself), expand to every site within the global maximum read distance of a changed site, re-enumerate each such site's enabled rules and resolved rates, and push its new total into the tree. With local chemistry the repair is O(z·r) work per step, independent of lattice size, against the legacy O(N·z) full rebuild.One honest caveat from reading the code: the deduplication marking array for the dirty set is a freshly allocated full-lattice Vec<bool> every step, so the actual per-step cost carries a Θ(N) zeroing term. The correctness rationale (deterministic insertion order for RNG-stable refresh) is sound; a persistent buffer with generation stamps would preserve it. Distance-2 queries also heap-allocate their first shell. Neither matters at 10⁴ sites; both would at 10⁶.

Floating-point drift, three defences

Incrementally maintained sums drift, and in a stiff system they can fail catastrophically. The tree defends itself at three levels, each aimed at a different failure. First, the descent path and the prefix-sum path visit the same internal nodes in different orders, so at a boundary adjacent to zero-rate leaves one ulp of disagreement can strand the descent on a leaf with no probability mass; find snaps forward to the nearest nonzero leaf (the direction exact arithmetic would have taken), then backward, and returns nothing only if every leaf is zero. A randomised gate probes sparse workloads over sizes 1 to 100 with targets including R·(1 − 10⁻¹⁶). Second, the tree rebuilds from its leaves every 65,536 steps, which is also the cheap correctness oracle in tests. Third, before every step, if the total is non-positive while some site still holds a positive event, the tree rebuilds immediately. That guard exists because of a bug the muscovite deck found: a lumped surface-release rate of 10¹² s⁻¹ firing once beside surviving 10⁻⁶ s⁻¹ migration events cancelled the survivors out of the internal sums entirely, and the engine reported "events exist but all rates are zero". The regression test is a two-site deck with those two rates.

Beyond the tree, a --paranoid mode re-enumerates every site from scratch at each report cadence and compares both the total (to 10⁻⁹ relative) and the exact event vector against the incrementally maintained tables. It is the differential test that the locality analysis behind the dirty set is right, and it runs every 1,000 steps in the kaolinite, multilayer and Kossel dynamics gates.

Determinism as law

Same deck, same seed, same strategy: the same trajectory, bit for bit, on any machine, forever. The mechanisms are unglamorous and total. One PCG64 stream per engine. A separate initialisation stream seeded from the deck seed XOR a fixed salt (0x9E3779B97F4A7C15), with a documented draw order: pass order, then site index order, one draw per site that passed every other filter. Replica seeds by one of two deck-declared policies: increment (seed + k) or hash, a pinned splitmix64 mix with published test vectors (seed 42, replicas 0–2: 0x57E1FABA65107204, 0xF34FE9248C9342E5, 0x725395388690AE46).One divergence between the RFC and the code, for accuracy: the RFC specifies a salted dynamics stream (seed XOR a constant); the implementation seeds the dynamics stream directly from the seed. Only the initialisation stream is salted. The contract holds either way, since the salt is a fixed function of the seed, but a paper should describe what is built. The draw order within a step is part of the contract per strategy. No hash maps in hot paths (ordered maps and marking arrays instead); no f32 in rate or time arithmetic; no parallelism within a trajectory; no draw that does not come from the strategy's stream. Every strategy's entire draw sequence is enumerable from the deck.

The contract is enforced by pinned oracles: byte logs of (step, time bits, site, reaction) captured from the pre-refactor engine, 20,000 steps of the kaolinite deck (520,031 bytes), 20,000 steps of an 8,000-site Kossel crystal, and a complete natural Kossel trajectory of exactly 1,512 events to exhaustion. On mismatch the failure message decodes the first differing byte into an event index. Regenerating an oracle requires a separate binary that refuses to run unless asked with a specific flag; the tests themselves are read-only. This is the discipline that made the Rust port of the C++ trustworthy, promoted from a porting technique to a platform law.

Section 12Defects and strain

Kinetic Monte Carlo owes its tractability to the repeated lattice; a dislocation is precisely a place where the tiling fails to close, and it carries a long-range elastic field. The resolution is that "defects" are three problems of sharply different difficulty, and the repeated lattice blocks only part of one of them.

The energetic problem (the strain field changes rates) needs no change to the lattice at all: strain energy enters as a per-site scalar field that modifies barriers. The topological problem (a screw dislocation changes connectivity) is exactly representable on an unbroken lattice by re-targeting bonds across a cut half-plane, the trick behind every Burton–Cabrera–Frank spiral-growth KMC. The self-consistent problem (strain that relaxes as material dissolves) needs a mechanics solver coupled to KMC. Petra implements the first, has designed the second, and has deliberately arranged the seam so the third can land without touching the reaction language. The classic dissolution-at-dislocations literature (Lasaga and Blum; Cabrera and Levine; Frank's hollow core; Lasaga and Lüttge's stepwave) lives almost entirely in the first category, which is why it came first.

Isotropic linear elasticity gives the strain energy stored in the material at a site at perpendicular distance r from a dislocation line, integrated over the site's atomic volume Ω:

u(r) = min( ucap, Amax(r, rcore)² )A = μ b² Ω8π² (screw),   A = μ b² Ω8π²(1 − ν) (edge, θ-averaged)

with shear modulus μ, Burgers vector b and Poisson ratio ν; Ω is the cell volume divided by sites per cell, and GPa·Å³ converts to kcal/mol by 0.1439326. The core clamp at rcore (default b) is where continuum elasticity fails, and doubles as Frank's hollow-core cap. Distances are perpendicular to the line and minimum-image on periodic axes only. Multiple defects superpose. A deck may set A directly for illustration.

Strain energy is a state function of the solid, so for any reaction that removes material from site i, the reaction free energy shifts by exactly −ui; how that splits between forward and reverse barriers is a Brønsted–Evans–Polanyi transfer coefficient β:

Ea,fwd(i) = Ea,f0 − β ui,    Ea,rev(i) = Ea,r0 + (1 − β) ui

Any β gives correct thermodynamics; Blum and Lasaga's classic treatment is β = 1, all of it in the dissolution rate. Petra makes it a per-reaction deck choice: each rule declares a strain_scale s and the engine adds s·ucenter to the barrier, so a forward/reverse pair writes −β and +(1 − β) and the scales differ by exactly −1 regardless of β, which is the invariant a detailed-balance linter would check. The engine cost of a static field is zero: it is one more term in the barrier, fully compatible with incremental updates.

The designed topological route is worth stating because it costs nothing later. A screw dislocation with Burgers vector equal to a whole lattice translation along its line is pure topology: choose a half-plane bounded by the line and re-target every bond crossing it to the equivalent site displaced by b. Every site keeps its template, coordination and states; only adjacency winds, so going once around the core advances one cell along the line. Petra's mechanics would be a post-build rewiring pass over the CSR arrays with the constraint that b is an integer number of cells. Edge dislocations change the site count and have no canonical stitching; stacking faults are a region-scoped bond-template override plus an areal energy stamp. Kaolinite plates are famously defect-rich in stacking rather than in line defects, which is the open question that decides which of these matters first; the four-layer kaolinite deck with labelled interlayer hydrogen bonds exists to be the substrate for either.

Section 13Schedules, ensembles, observables

Piecewise-isothermal schedules

Laboratory step-heating is piecewise isothermal by construction, and Track E needed it, so the CTMC strategy accepts an ordered list of (temperature, duration) segments. A segment boundary is a wall-time event in simulated time, not a reaction. The engine primitive advances until a deadline: it draws the site and event and the waiting time first, and if the wait would cross the boundary it discards both draws, jumps the clock exactly to the boundary, recompiles every temperature-dependent rate (including the chemical-potential coupling), refreshes every site, rebuilds the tree, and draws afresh at the new temperature. Discarding a drawn wait looks wrong until one recalls that the exponential distribution is memoryless: conditioning on "no event before the boundary" leaves the residual wait exponential with the new rate. The gate that makes this concrete asserts that every event strictly before the first boundary is bitwise identical to an independent isothermal run at the first temperature, and that a second segment at a temperature where a previously underflowed rate comes alive agrees with 128 independent runs at that temperature to within two event counts in the mean.

Ensembles

Rayon parallelises across replicas only; a replica is one serial engine and one serial RNG stream, and indexed collection restores replica order before aggregation so thread scheduling cannot alter output. Every replica records the initial sample, cadence samples and the final state even when the stop falls between cadences (a gate demands sample steps exactly [0, 5, 7] for seven steps at cadence five). Summary statistics are a deterministic percentile bootstrap with 2,000 resamples from a separate seed derived from the base seed, so the statistics cannot consume simulation randomness.

Observables

Observation is read-only, RNG-free and deterministic in site and adjacency order. Six kinds are implemented. State counts per dense state id. Event rates: the instantaneous total propensity per rule. Rate spectra: the distribution of positive per-site propensities, which is the Fischer–Lüttge observable, and the quantity Track A's second hypothesis is written in. Cluster sizes: connected components over a state set. Surface area as two deliberately separate measures: projected geometric area (occupied surface-normal columns counted once, times the face area from the cell vectors) and a BET-like proxy (every exposed solid site, including pit walls and internal roughness); the observable never divides one by the other. And exposure age: for every currently exposed site, the time since it most recently became exposed.

Exposure age is trajectory state, not observer state. The timestamp lives on the lattice, is updated inside the same deterministic commit that applies an event's writes (recomputed for the changed site and its immediate neighbours), is preserved while a site stays continuously exposed, and clears on burial. Surface age is therefore replayable and ensemble-aggregable rather than reconstructed by a reporter; a unit gate walks a four-site chain through exposure, burial at t = 1, and re-exposure at t = 4. The first customer was the aging smoke test for the field–lab program, discussed under Track A; the rate-spectrum observable has its own golden, pinned on the Kossel etch-pit deck: the log₁₀ standard deviation of the per-site propensity distribution broadens from 0.847 to 1.142 over the first 200 steps as the pit nucleates.

Section 14Verification

Petra cannot be validated by bitwise parity against the 1999 model: different algorithm, different float width, and it deliberately does not reproduce the legacy out-of-bounds phantom. The replacement is a ladder, and every rung is a test that runs in continuous integration.

The conformance rung deserves its numbers. Conway's Life on an 8×8 Moore torus under the synchronous strategy: after exactly four steps the live set equals the initial glider shifted by (+1, +1), and the clock reads exactly 4.0. The two-dimensional Ising model under Metropolis: the Binder cumulant U = 1 − ⟨m⁴⟩/3⟨m²⟩² at L = 8 and 12, ten replicas per temperature, 300L² burn-in sweeps and 500 sampling sweeps, crosses at T = 2.273230 against Onsager's exact 2.269185, an absolute error of 0.004 inside a stated tolerance of 0.25.The Ising deck is written in a strictly kcal/mol engine by exploiting the gas constant: energy.delta = −8R and per_match.dea = +4R, so acceptance exp(−ΔE/RT) cancels R and the deck expresses the standard reduced-unit coupling ΔE = 4n↑ − 8. The declared temperature is then literally Onsager's 2.269185 K. A lattice SIR epidemic under the probabilistic cellular automaton: the interpolated growth threshold sits at R0 = 1.017 against the mean-field 1, and the probability of a macroscopic outbreak (≥ 10 % of the lattice) is 0.000 at R0 = 0.5 and 0.477 at R0 = 2.0 over 128 hash-seeded replicas. The Ising gate is also run a second time through the parallel ensemble framework as its first customer.

Section 15Outputs, replay, the browser

A run writes a populations time series (CSV, one column per state), a long-form observables table, and for ensembles a per-seed finals table and a summary with means, 95 % bootstrap intervals and the full distribution. With --viz it also writes two artefacts for the sibling graph viewer: a PGIF snapshot and a JSONL event log. The snapshot is a single JSON document with columnar node arrays (Cartesian positions from the cell matrix, occupant type, state, kind, frozen flag, strain when any defect exists) and edge arrays with a seam flag for bonds that wrap a periodic boundary. All sites are emitted, vacant included, so a trajectory player can flip occupancy without adding or removing nodes.

The event log's first line is a header (deck, seed, site count, state and reaction dictionaries); every other line is one compact array: step, time, reaction, and a list of (site, old state, new state) deltas. Recording the old state makes the log invertible: the viewer scrubs backward by replaying deltas in reverse, and the test does exactly that, asserting the replay lands on the exact initial state. Rows are hand-formatted rather than serialised through a generic library to keep 10⁵-event logs cheap.

The same engine compiles to WebAssembly. WasmSim takes deck TOML and a seed, compiles in the browser, and exposes snapshot, header and step_batch(n), so the viewer's live mode generates trajectory events on demand and play/scrub means simulate. Because the engine is IEEE f64 and PCG64 throughout, a (deck, seed) pair reproduces the same trajectory in the browser as in the native binary. The WASM path fails closed on non-CTMC strategies and on scheduled decks until the trajectory format encodes temperature-boundary records.

Section 16Performance envelope

What is there: O(log N) selection consuming exactly two draws regardless of lattice size; incremental event maintenance over the dirty set; dense ids and bitsets with no strings or hashing in the hot loop; structure-of-arrays site storage and CSR adjacency; reused scratch buffers threaded through every neighbourhood query; per-site event vectors recycled with mem::take; rules pre-grouped by kind so a refresh never scans the whole rule table; the solution-coupling term materialised in log space; Rayon across replicas. What is not there: no release profile beyond cargo defaults (no LTO, no target-cpu), no benchmarks, and no published throughput numbers. The largest lattices exercised anywhere are 6,240 sites (the four-layer kaolinite), 8,000 (the 20³ Kossel oracle) and 32,768 (the muscovite phase-1 decks). Milestone P6, a 10⁶-site lattice at interactive step rates, is unstarted, and the handoff notes say plainly that the incremental updates "should scale" and that this is unproven.

The known algorithmic limit is stiffness. Fast reversible pairs waste steps flip-flopping, and the field–lab program needs years of simulated time against microsecond events. The plan is detection first (report the top rate-sum consumers), then superbasin escape or first-passage methods after the aging study produces a concrete stiffness profile, and tau-leaping as a mode on the exact strategy. The ice-mantle deck (Track D) sidesteps the problem for now by compressing its activated-process scale, and says so in its header.

III

Quarry, the rate pipeline

The quantum-chemistry half of the dissertation, built as orchestration rather than as an engine. Quarry cuts a terminated, mechanically embedded cluster out of the deck's own crystal, finds and verifies the saddle point of a reaction on it, reduces the result to transition-state-theory numbers with a tunnelling correction, and emits them as a deck fragment with provenance. Everything heavy is assembled from PySCF, GPU4PySCF, geomeTRIC, Sella and ASE; what is genuinely in-house is the cluster builder, the stage sequencing, the fifty-line rate module, the emitters, the provenance store and the gates.

Section 17What quarry computes

Given a site family in the KMC taxonomy (Al octahedra, Si tetrahedra, Si–O–Si bridges, Si–O–Al₂ bridges, Al–OH–Al bridges), a protonation state and a connectivity, quarry returns a site-resolved activation free energy and rate constant for one elementary reaction, with every intermediate structure content-addressed.

The stage sequence below is the Phase-2 ladder driver, one invocation per cell of the (family × protonation × connectivity) grid. A subtlety worth stating for anyone reading the code: the module named pipeline is the electronic-structure rung layer (energy, gradient, optimise, frequencies over PySCF), not the orchestrator. The end-to-end sequence is written longhand in the campaign scripts, and every stage is checkpointed as XYZ so a crashed campaign resumes where it died.

Section 18Clusters cut from the deck's crystal

The crystallography arrives through the KMC deck, not a CIF. Quarry reads the [cell] section of the petra kaolinite deck with the standard library's TOML parser (26 sites, 40 bonds, the sheared cell as a 3×3 matrix with columns as lattice vectors) and builds a bidirectional periodic adjacency with negated image offsets on the reverse edges. A unit test parses the 1999 model's data.cell directly and asserts that the site-kind list and the entire adjacency set with image offsets match the deck. Deck and QM cluster can therefore never silently disagree about the mineral.

Carving proceeds in seven steps. Center. The first site of the requested kind, or an explicit index; for a bridging oxygen the attacked metal is its first silicon partner. Connectivity pruning. The ladder's n is the number of intact bridges on the attacked metal in Q-notation; pruned bridges have their partner metals dissolved and survive as terminal hydroxyls, with edge-sharing octahedra treated as unprunable and a shortfall logged as a deviation rather than accepted silently. Shells. Breadth-first over metals to a depth of two shells (the default), then every bridging oxygen adjacent to an included metal at its crystallographic position. Termination by Pauling bond valence alone. Each oxygen wants a valence of 2; silicon contributes 1.0 per bond, aluminium 0.5, and the Al–OH–Al oxygen carries one structural proton. The integer part of the deficit becomes hydroxyl protons; the fractional residual is kept for the next step. No chemistry tables, no per-family special cases. Charge balance. To reach the target formal charge, protons are added at the most under-saturated oxygens farthest from the center first, or removed from the most over-saturated, and the attacked metal and bridging oxygen are excluded from both candidate lists: the charge you needed cannot perturb the bond you are breaking. Failure to reach the target raises. Proton placement. 0.96 Å along tilted directions (25° for OH, ±52.25° for OH₂ so the angle is 104.5°); topology only, everything is optimised afterwards. Freezing. Heavy atoms in the outermost metal shell are frozen at crystallographic positions; constructed protons are never frozen, because their positions are guesses, not crystallography.

Placing the attacker is harder in a crowded crystallographic cluster than on a free dimer. The attacking oxygen goes 3.2 Å from the attacked metal at 80° off the metal→bridge axis with one O–H aimed at the bridging oxygen; the azimuth is searched over 24 positions for maximum clearance and the polar angle widens through 80, 70, 90, 60, 100, 110° until clearance reaches 2.0 Å, raising below 1.6 Å. A caveat inherited from the source data: the legacy cell's bond lengths run 1.38–2.88 Å against real kaolinite's 1.6 (Si–O) and 1.9 Å (Al–O), and the frozen shell keeps those strained positions forever. The free region re-optimises, so connectivity trends (ΔΔEa across n) are safer than absolute barriers until the cell is re-derived from a modern refinement.

Section 19The electronic-structure stack

One engine, PySCF, with GPU4PySCF selected by a single boolean in a settings dataclass: functional, basis, optional solvent (SMD or PCM), optional dispersion (D3(BJ) or D4), grid level, density fitting, GPU. The SCF iteration cap is raised from PySCF's 50 to 150 because hand-built guesses and stretched scan geometries need it. Geometry optimisation drives geomeTRIC through PySCF's solver with frozen atoms passed as a constraint block written to a temporary file (geomeTRIC takes a path, not text; the error that taught this was a file-not-found raised on the constraint string itself). Hessians are analytic. Free clusters get PySCF's projected harmonic analysis; frozen clusters get a partial-Hessian vibrational analysis (PHVA): the free-atom block of the Hessian is symmetrised, mass-weighted and diagonalised without translational or rotational projection, since the frozen lattice removes those motions, and imaginary eigenvectors are re-embedded as full arrays that are exactly zero on frozen atoms. PHVA is automatic and invisible to everything downstream.

Section 20Finding the right saddle

A verified saddle is not necessarily the right saddle. In one evening the pipeline produced three transition states that passed every mechanical test (one imaginary mode, converged IRC) and were chemically wrong. The gates exist because of that evening, and each one has a receipt.

The search is layered. A relaxed constrained scan drives one distance (the attacking oxygen toward the metal) while everything else, minus the frozen shell, relaxes at each point. The transition-state guess is the first interior maximum, not the global maximum: once the driven bond forms, pushing further compresses it and the energy rises without bound, so the global maximum lands on the compression wall (measured live: crest at 1.96 Å, bond formation, then a wall that out-climbed the crest). If the scan ends with energy still rising, the ridge was never crossed, and the pipeline refuses to hand the endpoint to a saddle search; instead it extends the scan in 0.08 Å steps down to a 1.60 Å floor and, failing that, raises an error that carries the completed scan so the caller can pivot to a second driven coordinate without paying twice. The receipt for that refusal: an uncrossed scan endpoint relaxed by Sella into a 208i cm⁻¹ water-wag saddle with a 2.5 kJ/mol "barrier" against a literature value near 120, mechanically valid and chemically nonsense. Scan points more than 40 kJ/mol above their neighbours' interpolation are re-optimised from both neighbouring structures and the lower kept; a point reproduced high from both sides is a real sharp crest and is attempted once.

The saddle itself comes from Sella, a partitioned rational-function optimiser following one uphill mode in internal coordinates, over an ASE calculator that wraps PySCF (the adapter's forces are tested against central finite differences to 10⁻³ eV/Å, a real sign-unit-coordinate gate). When an undirected search slides off the target, as it did twice on the pilot cell (into the pentacoordinate intermediate), the recovery is a directed Cartesian search: compute the reactant-to-product displacement with rigid rotation and translation removed, zero it on the frozen shell, mask it to the reactive core (bridge oxygen, attacked metal, water oxygen and its hydrogens) so terminal-hydroxyl rotations cannot choose the unstable direction, project it into Sella's free basis and install it as the initial mode. Climbing-image NEB with five images exists as a guess generator and fails closed on an unconverged band; at sixty-plus atoms it cost five minutes per band step and seven hours to not converge, so the ladder driver prefers to re-cross the col from the product side with a checkpointable scan along the rupturing bond.

Verification requires exactly one imaginary mode above a noise floor (0 for free clusters, 30 cm⁻¹ for PHVA on frozen clusters, whose soft numerically imaginary modes are an artefact of the embedding). Then a quick-IRC: displace ±0.15 Å along the mode (0.50 and 1.00 Å in the dimer campaigns), relax both ways fully, and classify each endpoint by a basin signature, a tuple of bonding booleans at fixed thresholds (Si–Ow bonded below 2.3 Å, Si–Obr bonded below 2.3 Å, transferred proton on Obr below 1.25 Å) that identifies bonding topology without caring about conformer identity. The acid path uses a six-tuple that adds the opposite metal's bond and three proton counts by unique nearest-oxygen assignment, so a dissociated or missing proton cannot masquerade as a rotamer; that particular false-green was found by live optimisation, not review.

Publication is a transaction. Starting a gated run quarantines any prior results and store to timestamped superseded files and writes a status of running. A failed gate leaves no canonical output and writes failed; a model-validity failure writes blocked with a detail string. Canonical outputs are written to temporary files and atomically replaced only after every saddle gate, IRC gate, minimum check, conformer-equivalence check, thermochemistry and store close-out succeed. A blocked run leaves no results file at all, so a stale success cannot be read by accident. One test parametrises the three terminal failure classes and asserts not only the recorded detail but the number of Hessian and IRC calls made before stopping ((0, 0), (1, 0), (1, 1)): fail-closed also means fail-cheap.

Section 21From frequencies to rate constants

The rate module is deliberately small (about three hundred lines including the Eckart integral) and checked against closed-form values to 10⁻¹² rather than borrowed, with GoodVibes and Arkane named as the audited references it is meant to agree with. Units: kJ/mol, kelvin, cm⁻¹, s⁻¹; CODATA 2018 constants.

Quasi-rigid-rotor harmonic-oscillator thermochemistry. Zero-point energy is ½Σhcν. Vibrational thermal energy stays purely harmonic. Vibrational entropy uses Grimme's interpolation between the harmonic oscillator and a free rotor of moment μ′ = μBav/(μ + Bav) with Bav = 10⁻⁴⁴ kg m², damped by

w(ν) = 11 + (ν0/ν)⁴,   ν0 = 100 cm⁻¹;    Svib = Σ [w SHO + (1 − w) SFR]

Translation is Sackur–Tetrode at 1 bar, rotation uses the rotational temperatures with a symmetry number (none for a PHVA cluster, which does not rotate), and H = Eel + ZPE + Ethermal + RT, G = H − TS. Imaginary modes are handled by contract, not filtering: the thermochemistry raises if handed a non-positive frequency, and the caller drops the reaction mode and passes it to the tunnelling correction instead. Translational terms are still computed for embedded clusters and cancel in any barrier between states of identical composition, which is the only legitimate use of PHVA results.A trap worth recording: PySCF's own thermochemistry silently detects the molecular point group and divides the rotational partition function by the symmetry number, so an independent implementation with σ = 1 differs by exactly R ln σ in entropy (5.76 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ for water). The cross-check test passes the matching σ and agrees to a relative 10⁻³.

Eyring and tunnelling.

k(T) = κ(T) kBTh exp(−ΔG‡/RT),    κWigner = 1 + u²24,   u = hc|ν‡|kBT

The asymmetric Eckart correction follows Johnston and Heicklen as implemented in Arkane: forward and reverse ZPE-corrected barriers V₁, V₂ and the imaginary frequency define α₁ = 2πV₁/hν, α₂ = 2πV₂/hν; if α₁α₂ ≤ π²/4 the barrier is too thin for the Eckart form and the code falls back to Wigner. Otherwise the transmission probability T(E) is integrated on a 20,000-point grid from max(0, V₁ − V₂) to V₁ + 40kBT against the Boltzmann weight, in a form divided through by ea+b so no hyperbolic cosine ever overflows. The forward Eckart barrier is built from electronic energy plus zero-point energy only, never from ΔG. Gates: zero barrier gives exactly kBT/h; argon's Sackur–Tetrode entropy 154.846 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ to ±0.01; Eckart agrees with Wigner within 2 % for a broad 150 cm⁻¹ barrier and tends to 1.0 ± 0.1 at 2,000 K.

Section 22The bridge and the provenance store

The emitter renders [[reactions]] entries in petra's own syntax with a miniature TOML writer (the standard library reads TOML but does not write it, and a dependency is not worth a closed set of shapes): a name, a center selector, optional guards and produced species, a rate as either {eyring = {dh, ds}} or {arrhenius = {prefactor, ea}}, then modifier blocks carrying a selector and a by_count table, then effects. The reaction mechanism (center, guards, effects) is deck knowledge passed through untouched; quarry owns only the numbers. Above every reaction and every modifier it writes a provenance comment: method, engine, the first twelve hex digits of the geometry hash, and the date. Splicing into a template replaces a marker line and refuses any template that does not declare kilojoules per mole, so a unit mismatch is structurally impossible rather than documented. The test suite compiles and runs the emitted deck with the real petra CLI for ten steps; a second emitter regenerates the 1999 model's data.rxn input verbatim, tab-separated barrier tables and all.

The store is plain SQLite with three tables (structures, jobs, results), no ORM and no services: one user, one box. A structure's key is the SHA-256 of its whitespace-canonicalised XYZ block, and adding the same geometry twice returns the existing row. Results upsert on (job, key). The context manager commits on clean exit and rolls back on exception, and a test proves the rollback. Big artefacts (checkpoints, cube files, trajectories) stay on disk outside git; the store keeps derived numbers, provenance and light XYZ. Beside it, every run directory carries a results JSON with barriers, tunnelling factors, imaginary modes, literature anchors and the driver's git commit, and the status file that says whether the canonical outputs are current.

The bridge carries barriers, never rates, for the reason given in Section 1: a tested 0.17 % disagreement between quarry's CODATA gas constant and petra's dissertation-era value. Emitting ΔH‡ and ΔS‡ and letting the engine exponentiate keeps both codes honest about which constant they use.

Silicate dissolution and the discrepancy budget

The flagship is the field–lab discrepancy of Section 3, and the plan is built around six ranked, falsifiable hypotheses: (H1) inventory depletion reproduces the parabolic decline of rate with surface age; (H2) the rate spectrum narrows and shifts down with age while its mode barely moves, explaining lab-to-lab irreproducibility; (H3) the two-regime defect dependence that reproduces Blum, Yund and Lasaga's null result far from equilibrium and defect control near it; (H4) the roughness paradox falls out because new area created by etching is low-energy area; (H5) annealing moves accelerate aging beyond pure depletion; (H6, stretch) grinding resets the clock with a computable half-life, the number enhanced-rock-weathering accounting actually needs. Phase 0, the observables those hypotheses are written in (rate spectra, two surface areas, exposure age), landed in Section 13. Its smoke test is the first picture of the flagship.

The step at which the propensity collapses is also where the rate spectrum collapses: its log₁₀ standard deviation goes from 0 (all fast sites identical) through 1.44 (fast and slow coexisting) to 0 again (only terrace sites left), which is H2 in miniature. A gate demands that the defect inventory be exactly zero by step 180 and the summed event rate below 10 % of its initial value. The phased plan from here is a generic aging study on Kossel-family decks with finite defect inventories and annealing channels (publishable as a mechanism paper on its own), the QM-grounded kaolinite deck once the ladder lands, and then the attribution capstone: a stacked budget of log units from inventory depletion, defect exhaustion at field undersaturation, annealing and the normalisation artefact, set against the log units the transport literature assigns to hydrology. The honest structural limit is stated in the scoping document: petra has no amorphous interphase and no coupled reprecipitation film, so where its budget falls short of the observed decline, the residual is an estimate of the coating and transport channels' share.

A community by-product: the kinetics database

Palandri and Kharaka's 2004 USGS compilation remains the field's standard rate table and is PDF-bound. An agent transcribed its silicate core into 36 machine-readable mineral records carrying 74 rate-law mechanisms, validated by a dependency-free script that rejects malformed TOML, missing fields, non-finite parameters, dangling provenance (every numeric value must reference the record's page-and-table source) and an inventory below 25 minerals. The transcription is deliberately conservative: the surface-area basis is unspecified in 35 of 36 records because the summary tables do not state one after pooling primary studies (quartz alone is labelled BET); empty reaction orders mean "not printed", not zero; empty uncertainty lists mean "not reported", not zero uncertainty. The kaolinite record carries a caveat rather than a preference: the compilation's fitted acid and base activation energies (65.9 and 17.9 kJ/mol) lie outside the experimental ranges the survey quotes (≈29 acidic, 33–51 alkaline), and a pooled mechanism-specific fit is not interchangeable with an individual apparent Ea.

Ccorrosion

Pitting corrosion

Is the localised corrosion of passive metals mathematically the dissertation's problem in a hard hat, and can exact stochastic dynamics with ensemble statistics reproduce the distributions the field measures but cannot derive?

The bet is an isomorphism. Pitting is localised attack at defects, a film-breakdown-versus-repair race, stochastic initiation and evolving pit morphology: every element has a counterpart in defect-driven mineral dissolution, and the corrosion modelling literature (mean-field point-defect models, ad hoc cellular automata, phase field) lacks exactly what petra brings, namely exact continuous-time dynamics, environment-dependent barriers with provenance, and ensemble statistics matched to experimental distributions (Weibull induction times, near-normal pitting potentials, Gumbel maximum pit depths). The Kossel etch-pit deck of Section 12 is already a pitting simulation in every respect but vocabulary. Stakes are stated with the industry's own number: about US$2.5 trillion a year in corrosion cost, of which the localised fraction is the unpredictable part.

The minimal viable deck is a two-dimensional grid of film patches (sites are nanometre-scale patches, not atoms; the coarse graining is stated up front and is the Shibata and point-defect-model tradition). Six transitions: chloride adsorption at a rate proportional to activity and exp(αFE/RT), its detailed-balanced desorption, film thinning with a by_count modifier in which each thinned or bare neighbour lowers the barrier (autocatalysis, the model's heart), strain-coupled rupture seeded at weak sites (the inclusion analogue), metal dissolution with bond-counting undercut barriers, and repassivation whose rate falls with active-neighbour count. Electrode potential enters exactly as the existing chemical-potential knob. It runs under the original CTMC strategy, so the showcase also proves the dissertation's algorithm in a new domain, complementing the three new strategies of Section 14.

Dastrochemistry

Grain-surface chemistry at 10 kelvin

Can quarry supply provenance-carrying, tunnelling-corrected rate coefficients to databases that are 60 % educated guesses, and can petra be the first open, declarative microscopic grain-surface Monte Carlo?

Interstellar ice chemistry runs on rate networks in which about a fifth of the coefficients are measured, a sixth calculated, and the rest estimated (KIDA's 2024 network carries 7,667 gas-phase reactions over 584 species). JWST's ice inventories have made the models the bottleneck: a 2025 five-code comparison found the codes diverge sharply below a dust temperature of 12 K. Egeochronology

Argon release from muscovite

What does ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar step-heating of muscovite actually measure, when the crystal is falling apart as it heats?

Sletten and Onstott (1998) showed experimentally that in-vacuo argon release from muscovite below about 900 °C is rate-limited by dehydroxylation and delamination rather than volume diffusion: radiogenic ⁴⁰Ar* lives in interlayer vacancies, atmospheric ³⁶Ar in extended defects, the two reservoirs have apparent diffusivities differing by up to five orders early in a run, and the "diffusion profiles" read from age spectra may be artefacts of decomposition. Nteme and colleagues (2022–2024) computed the ideal-lattice side: a divacancy interlayer mechanism with a migration enthalpy near 66 kcal/mol, too slow to explain measured mobility, and sub-grain retentive cores separated by zones where diffusivity is enhanced by up to six orders. The empirical argument and the ideal-lattice theory agree from opposite directions, and nobody has built the middle: a lattice whose structure evolves while argon migrates through it with siting-specific barriers under a laboratory temperature staircase. Petra can express nearly all of it, and the one engine feature it lacked, piecewise-isothermal schedules, was built for it (Section 13).

Phase 1 (E1) used published numbers only: divacancy hop 66 kcal/mol, bulk dehydroxylation 57 kcal/mol (the value the 1998 paper used), and a steric slowdown table 57/60/69 kcal/mol for zero, one or two residual-oxygen neighbours, labelled a bracketing envelope spanning the modern in-situ range rather than measured barriers. The deck is 32×32 independent c-axis columns of eight 2M₁ repeats, 32,768 sites, gallery seeded with 94.5 % vacancies, 5 % ⁴⁰Ar and 0.5 % immobile potassium (831 argon atoms at seed 1998), open c boundary with a lumped 1 s⁻¹ surface release. Each synthetic release curve was inverted through the Hames–Bowring infinite-cylinder series the 1998 paper used.

The reaction-direction counts balance within about 5 % across the four hop classes (8,192 dehydroxylation events; 4,390–4,623 hops per class), so the shape is not a topology artefact of the alternating gallery chain, whose intra- and inter-cell hops carry distinct bond labels precisely to avoid the first-match directional bias of Section 8. This deck is also the one that found the Fenwick cancellation bug of Section 11: its original 10¹² s⁻¹ lumped release rate erased the 10⁻⁶ s⁻¹ migration events from the tree.

Phase 2 (E2) adds the rest of the 1998 mechanism as explicit states: extended-defect zones with a 40 kcal/mol proxy hop (from the six-order enhancement), an interface that transitions from bonded to delaminated (58 kcal/mol proxy) once its slab has dehydroxylated, three argon isotopes as separate species with masses, and octahedral recoil traps for ³⁹Ar (64 kcal/mol escape proxy). The 890-line deck is generated byte for byte by a script with a drift-rejecting test. A compact 4×4×6-cell volume (768 sites; 12 ⁴⁰Ar, 38 ³⁹Ar of which 34 in traps, 1 ³⁶Ar) was run under a five-segment schedule, 500 to 700 °C in 50 °C steps of 600 s, completing in 277 events; two runs at the same seed produced byte-identical products across all five boundaries.

Two further results landed after the first edition. The grain-size sweep (E2b, 2026-08-28) answered the obvious objection to a 768-site demonstration: run as 32 deterministic replicas at seven volumes from 768 to 559,872 sites, the old-first-step staircase stabilises at 49,152 sites (16×16×24 cells) under a predeclared gate on release fractions, step ages and defined-age fractions, and stays stable through an 11-fold further volume increase; same-seed replay is byte-identical at every size, and the next rung (1.3 million sites) was memory-killed, so 559,872 stands as the host's evidence-backed ceiling. At the largest volume the five-step spectrum is 38.3, 21.3, 0.4, 0 and 0 Ma with tight bootstrap bands, and one honest negative: the mechanism does not produce a large evolving ³⁶Ar/⁴⁰Ar step ratio at this tier. The classical barrier campaign (E3a, 2026-09-03) is phase 3 begun. A source-constrained input contract rebuilt Nteme's 1,512-atom ClayFF muscovite with a charge-correct reconstruction of the undisclosed remote compensators; six eight-image LAMMPS climbing-image NEBs then reproduced the published route-1 divacancy barrier to +1.1 % (68.41 versus 67.64 kcal/mol), computed a post-dehydroxylation argon hop at 64.10 kcal/mol (inside the deck's 57–69 envelope) and a xenon screen at 90.74, and contradicted the deck's 40 kcal/mol extended-zone proxy for the three lens geometries tested (all three came out at 68.41, indistinguishable from the pristine gallery). Every barrier is typed incomplete-convergence (final force norms three to six times the requested threshold, though the barriers moved by at most 0.01 kcal/mol over the last 500 steps); the octahedral-trap seed left its cage and remains proxy-retained; delamination has no atom-resolved endpoint yet. The deck was deliberately not mutated: its schema cannot carry a typed convergence state beside a live barrier without making the number look production-ready.

What remains of phase 3: a convergence-tightening or narrow schema card so E3a's numbers can ride in a deck with their state attached, periodic-DFT spot checks (E3b) on the five-coordinate aluminium environments where classical potentials are least trustworthy, and then the point of the track (E4): synthetic spectra from computed barriers at E2b-validated volumes laid over the six named 1998 figures and Villa's isotope-correlation test. Scale is stated honestly throughout: a 3 mm disk is 10¹⁹ atoms and petra's practical range is 10⁶–10⁸ sites, which is usefully the half-micron dehydroxylate-lamella scale the 1998 slab model inferred.

Apparatus

How the work gets done, and the vocabulary and sources a reader may want at hand.

Section 25How the program runs

The labour is done by a small fleet of AI agents (cloud workers, local workers, two otherwise idle laptops) coordinating through nothing but the git repository. Work lives on a card board under docs/program/: a plan of what is ready, a protocol for claiming and delivering, a rolling status log, and one file per card. An agent claims a card by pushing a branch named for it, which is atomic by git's own rules; the work, the card update and the status line land in one reviewed pull request; a cloud agent runs the merge ceremony, including the requirement that every automated-review thread be resolved before merge. There is no server, no scheduler and no meeting. In the program's first thirty hours the board processed the quantum pipeline's construction, the engine generalisation, the astrochemistry campaign and the kinetics database across three different agent runtimes, two of which found and reported their own failures before a human looked.

Four rules recur in every card and were each learned the hard way. Barriers cross tool boundaries, never rates (the gas-constant assertion of Section 22). Every emitted number carries provenance: method, geometry hash, date. Every engine change reproduces the past bit for bit (the oracles of Section 11), and a merged fix is not done until its activation was verified live. A verified saddle is not necessarily the right saddle, so structural gates stay on, and a negative result is written up with the same receipts as a positive one. Big outputs (checkpoints, trajectories, cube files) stay out of git; derived numbers and their provenance go in the store and the ledger. Compute etiquette (GPU first, CPU stages capped at sixteen threads, everything teed to a file, no multi-hour full-CPU job without an explicit go) is enforced in code where it can be and written down where it cannot.

AppendixGlossary

Kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC)
Exact stochastic simulation of a continuous-time Markov chain over discrete states: events fire one at a time with probability proportional to their rates, and time advances by exponentially distributed waits.
Rejection-free / n-fold way / BKL
The Bortz–Kalos–Lebowitz scheme that draws the next event directly from the propensity distribution rather than proposing and rejecting. Gillespie's direct method is the chemical-kinetics name for the same idea.
Propensity
The rate of an event given the current state; the sum over all enabled events is the total propensity R, whose reciprocal is the mean waiting time.
Fenwick tree
A binary indexed tree over an array that supports point updates and prefix sums in O(log N); here, over per-site total propensities, to select a site in O(log N).
Influence radius
The graph distance from a fired event within which any other site's enabled events could have changed: effect reach plus maximum guard read distance. Defines the dirty set.
Deck
Petra's TOML model document: structure, dynamics, execution, observables.
Guard / modifier / effect
A rule's precondition on the neighbourhood, its environment-dependent barrier adjustment, and its state rewrites.
by_count
A tabulated barrier increment indexed by the count of matching neighbours; the general, nonlinear form of bond counting.
Transition-state theory (TST)
Rate constant from the free-energy difference between reactant and the saddle point of the potential-energy surface: k = (kBT/h) exp(−ΔG‡/RT) (Eyring).
Saddle point / transition state
A stationary point with exactly one negative Hessian eigenvalue, hence one imaginary vibrational frequency, along the reaction coordinate.
IRC
Intrinsic reaction coordinate: the steepest-descent path from the saddle to the two minima it connects. Quarry's quick-IRC displaces along the imaginary mode and relaxes both ways instead of integrating the path.
Quasi-RRHO
Rigid-rotor harmonic-oscillator thermochemistry with Grimme's interpolation of low-frequency entropies toward a free rotor, removing the harmonic divergence.
Wigner / Eckart
Tunnelling corrections to a classical rate: Wigner is a small-parameter expansion in the imaginary frequency; Eckart integrates transmission through an analytic asymmetric barrier.
PHVA
Partial Hessian vibrational analysis: diagonalise only the free-atom block of the Hessian for a cluster whose outer shell is frozen.
Lattice resistance
The increase of a bond-rupture barrier in an embedded, constrained cluster relative to a free molecular fragment; Pelmenschikov's term.
Brønsted–Evans–Polanyi (BEP)
The empirical linear relation between a barrier change and the reaction-energy change; the transfer coefficient β is the slope.
Cabrera–Levine / Frank hollow core
The undersaturation threshold for an etch pit to open at a dislocation, and the energetic emptiness of a dislocation core when elastic energy exceeds surface energy.
Rate spectrum
The distribution of dissolution rates over the sites of a surface (Fischer, Arvidson, Lüttge); the observable that area normalisation destroys.
BET vs geometric area
Gas-adsorption surface area, which counts internal roughness, versus projected footprint. Their ratio is the roughness factor.
Binder cumulant
U = 1 − ⟨m⁴⟩/3⟨m²⟩²; its curves for different lattice sizes cross at the critical temperature.
Density fitting (DF / RI)
Approximating four-index electron-repulsion integrals through an auxiliary basis; the production path on the GPU.
Golden gate
A test that pins current output against a stored reference produced by a trusted earlier version or code.
