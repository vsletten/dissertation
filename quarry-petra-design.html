Quarry + Petra — From Ab Initio Barriers to Lattice Kinetics

Quarry + Petra · Science brief

From ab initio barriers to exact lattice kinetics

The platform couples electronic-structure kinetics to stochastic lattice dynamics. Quarry computes site-resolved transition-state-theory rate constants on terminated aluminosilicate clusters; Petra propagates them by rejection-free kinetic Monte Carlo on the declared crystal. It is the classic QM → TST → KMC architecture rebuilt as one instrument — with the software engineering explained along the way for readers who don't live in it.

The rate side: Quarry

Barriers come from cluster calculations, tiered so that expensive theory is spent only on structures that survive cheap theory: GFN2-xTB pre-screening (with CREST for conformer and water-placement sampling) → DFT geometry optimization with analytic frequencies → transition states located by relaxed scans and CI-NEB, refined with Sella, and verified by IRC → thermochemistry via quasi-RRHO → Eyring, with Wigner or Eckart tunneling corrections. Eckart is not a nicety here: for the low-temperature astrochemical applications below, tunneling is the rate. A consumer GPU (RTX 4090 with GPU4PySCF) makes the frequency-heavy TST workflow routine at cluster sizes the original 1990s-era work could not touch.

DFT barriers are spot-checked against DLPNO-CCSD(T), which is itself cross-validated against canonical CCSD(T) run on an independent GPU implementation — the two coupled-cluster engines agree to ~10⁻⁸ Eh on shared tests, with DLPNO within ~0.5 kJ/mol of canonical.

Anchor result. Neutral Si–O–Si hydrolysis on a disilicic-acid model (four-center water-attack mechanism): ΔG‡(298 K) = 113.0 kJ/mol = 27.0 kcal/mol at B3LYP/def2-SVP, against Xiao & Lasaga's ~29 kcal/mol — one imaginary mode at the saddle, proton delivered in the TS, IRC-connected. Stated caveats: double-ζ basis, gas phase, quick-IRC on a soft mode. The production tier (r²SCAN-3c geometries; ωB97M-V/def2-TZVPD + SMD single points; D4 dispersion) is specified and staged.

The target is not one number but the ladder: barriers resolved by connectivity and protonation state for each bridging-site family (Si–O–Si, Si–O–Al, Al–OH–Al), emitted as tabulated ΔEa versus coordination.

The dynamics side: Petra

Petra is a rejection-free (n-fold way / BKL) kinetic Monte Carlo engine: exact sampling of the master equation, no rejected moves, stochastic time steps from the total rate. The physics commitments that matter:

  • Detailed balance is enforced, not assumed. Reverse rules are generated with k⁻ = k⁺·exp(ΔE/RT), and a load-time linter searches each site kind's state graph for closed thermodynamic cycles whose ΣΔE ≠ 0. The classic silent KMC sin — a model that pumps free energy out of nothing — becomes a loud warning with the offending cycle printed.
  • Solution coupling via activities and chemical potentials. Attachment rates carry the standard a·exp(Δμ/RT) supersaturation factor; v1 treats the solution as an infinite reservoir with fixed activities.
  • Environment dependence is data, and nonlinear by default. ΔEa versus neighbor count is a tabulated ladder (by_count), not a forced linear bond-count slope. Published per-connectivity barriers — quartz Q1/Q2/Q3, for instance — drop straight into a deck.
  • Defects with elastic strain. Screw and edge dislocations are declared in the input; their analytic elastic fields enter local barriers with detailed-balance-consistent conventions. An etch-pit example drills a pit down a screw core, gated by ensemble tests.
  • Ensembles are first-class. A run is (deck, seed); an ensemble is a seed sweep with real statistics — not the single-trajectory animation much of the lattice-modeling literature still publishes.

What the engineering buys

Several design choices from the software side are worth translating, because they are what make the platform usable as an instrument rather than a codebase:

  • The model is one document. Structure, state vocabulary, mechanism, kinetics, thermodynamic conditions, and run specification all live in a single human-readable input deck (TOML — an indented plain-text format). The engine contains no chemistry; a different mineral or mechanism is a different deck run by the same binary. Decks can be diffed, versioned, and shared like manuscripts. The predecessor — the 1999 dissertation code this generalizes — hard-coded all of it, so every model change was a software project.
  • Exact reproducibility as law. The same deck and random seed produce the same trajectory, bit for bit, on every run. Replication means re-running a file, not re-implementing a method section.
  • Numbers carry receipts. Every rate constant traces back to its calculation — method, geometry, date — in a lightweight provenance database that travels with the results.
  • Verification you can inspect. The engine is gated against closed-form statistical mechanics (two-state equilibrium occupancies, Langmuir isotherms, waiting-time distributions); a paranoid mode recomputes all event bookkeeping from scratch and asserts agreement; and the kaolinite model is cross-checked site-by-site against an independently verified port of the original code.
  • You can watch. The engine compiles to WebAssembly and runs in an ordinary browser: edit the deck, re-seed, play and scrub the trajectory.

Where it's pointed

  • Kaolinite, closed-loop. Regenerate the dissertation model's rate table from computed k(T) and compare simulated dissolution kinetics against experimental activation energies (~29 kJ/mol acidic; 33–51 kJ/mol alkaline).
  • The field–lab discrepancy. Quantitative attribution of how much of the 2–4 missing orders of magnitude in field weathering rates is intrinsic surface evolution — defect depletion, surface aging, rate-spectrum narrowing — which the defect and ensemble machinery makes addressable for the first time.
  • Pitting corrosion. Ensemble pit-initiation-time statistics against published stainless-steel distributions. Localized attack at defects is the same mathematics as dissolution.
  • Grain-surface astrochemistry. Tunneling-dominated rates at 10–50 K for ice-mantle chemistry, in a field whose community databases openly flag order-of-magnitude guessed rates and accept computed submissions.
  • Ar release from muscovite. A lattice whose structure evolves (dehydroxylation, delamination) while Ar migrates with siting-specific barriers — toward synthetic ⁴⁰Ar/³⁹Ar step-heating spectra, testing a 1998 empirical interpretation by its own author.

Honest limits. The engine is on-lattice: no explicit solvent, no off-lattice relaxation (analytic defect strain fields are the deliberate exception). Stiff fast-reversible pairs are detected and reported but not yet accelerated. Current barriers are first-tier (double-ζ, gas phase); the production protocol is staged. Barriers are always inputs to the dynamics — Petra never computes them on the fly.
